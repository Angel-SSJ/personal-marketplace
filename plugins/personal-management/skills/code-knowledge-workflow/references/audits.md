# CODE audits and preventive checks

## Audit the flow, not the note collection

Start with a concrete symptom: overcapture, inability to retrieve relevant knowledge, repeated summarization without output, loss of meaning, unverifiable claims, or stalled expression. Inspect a bounded recent work episode and its material-to-output path. A large library or many unused notes is not sufficient evidence of failure.

Use these local intervention levels: an in-use check for the material currently being used; a focused audit for a recurring stage/handoff failure; a broader system audit only if focused repairs fail or several workflows are affected. Reuse the existing review rhythm. These levels and escalation triggers are local policies, not canonical CODE requirements.

## Evidence-based checks

| Stage/handoff | Question                                                           | Observable failure                                                               | Small repair                                     |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Capture       | Is collection supporting questions, resonance, or real use?        | Repeated irrelevant collection displaces committed work                          | Narrow the active capture filter                 |
| Organize      | Can relevant knowledge be found where it is used?                  | An active output repeatedly starts from scratch despite useful existing material | Link the relevant support into current work      |
| Distill       | Is effort invested when useful, with meaning intact?               | Bulk summaries consume the work session or erase qualifications                  | Distill a selected item at use; preserve context |
| Express       | Does knowledge lead to an intended contribution?                   | Research continues without addressing a named gap                                | Build one small useful output                    |
| Evidence      | Can a consequential claim be traced and checked?                   | An unattributed or contradictory summary is treated as settled fact              | Restore provenance and disclose the conflict     |
| Commitments   | Are actual next actions tracked independently?                     | A saved source or draft is treated as completion of a promised result            | Clarify the outcome and current next action      |
| Lifecycle     | Are reusable results accessible and inactive clutter out of focus? | Old outputs are repeatedly remade or stale facts are blindly reused              | Retrieve, recheck, and reuse one relevant packet |

Do not audit filename conventions or demand internal note sections. Ask whether the workflow helps the person think and produce work. Any proposed example output is an aid, not a required document structure.

For this user's storage profile, also inspect current drafts/packets in project docs, superseded iterations in drafts, exclusive sources in assets, frozen outputs in submissions, and intact archival/harvesting transitions. Report a role or lifecycle mismatch without inspecting arbitrary document sections or imposing a filename convention. Verify a proposed correction preserves delivered snapshots and source provenance.

## Finding and response format

Describe the evidence and inspected scope, locate the broken stage or handoff, explain its effect on actual work, and propose the smallest repair. Include how to retest it and what recurrence would justify another check. Label inferred causes and uninspected areas explicitly.

Prioritize misleading or unverifiable consequential claims first, then lost commitments and stalled outputs, then recurring processing waste. Optional aesthetic improvements are not methodological defects. Do not promise productivity gains from a snapshot or grade the person's worth by output volume.

## Verification and prevention

Replay the failed use: can the relevant claim be traced, the useful material found, or the small intended output made without a fresh research spiral? Distinguish proposed repair, actual execution, and verified outcome. Confirm that the fix reduced work rather than creating an obligation to maintain every capture.

Leave a cue at the failure point: a specific gap before collecting more, source access before relying on a summary, or a small expression target before another distillation pass. Check that cue during normal use. Broader audits are warranted when the same failure persists, the stakes change, or a major workflow transition invalidates the previous arrangement.

## Example finding

Observed: the last two report sessions were spent summarizing unrelated captures; no draft decision was advanced. Inference: distillation has become detached from the current purpose. Repair: choose one report question and produce a small supported comparison from existing material. Verification: the comparison helps the intended audience and its claims can be traced. Prevention: new research names the gap it will fill. Do not infer that all captured material is useless or that all future sessions need a full audit.
