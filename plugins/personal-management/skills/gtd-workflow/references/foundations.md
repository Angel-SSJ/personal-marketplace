# GTD foundations

## The five steps

David Allen's Getting Things Done separates collecting inputs from deciding what they mean and doing the work.

| Step     | Decision or practice                                                                      | Useful result                                                               |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Capture  | Externalize things that have attention or imply an open loop                              | Reliable collected inputs                                                   |
| Clarify  | Decide what an input is, whether it is actionable, and what the outcome/next action means | A decision rather than an ambiguous reminder                                |
| Organize | Put the resulting reminder where its meaning will be visible at the right time            | Trusted action, project, waiting, calendar, incubation, and reference views |
| Reflect  | Review and update the system against reality                                              | Current commitments and usable choices                                      |
| Engage   | Choose and do work with appropriate context and perspective                               | Progress on what matters now                                                |

These are recurring practices, not a one-time setup or an obligation to execute every action during inbox processing.

## Concepts and containers

| Concept                 | Meaning                                                                                   | Distinction                                                                           |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open loop               | Something incomplete that has attention                                                   | Can lead to an action, reference, incubation, or a decision to stop                   |
| Inbox                   | Temporary collection before clarification                                                 | Not a prioritized action list                                                         |
| Next action             | The next physical, visible step that can advance something                                | Different from a vague intention or multi-step outcome                                |
| Project                 | A desired outcome requiring more than one action, normally achievable within about a year | A longer ambition may need nearer projects; it is not a reason to delete the ambition |
| Project support         | Plans and information needed to achieve an outcome                                        | Not the master source of next-action reminders                                        |
| Next Actions            | Available actions for oneself with flexible timing                                        | Not a daily promise to finish every listed action                                     |
| Calendar                | Time-specific or day-specific actions and day-specific information                        | Different from a flexible task wish list                                              |
| Waiting For             | Work or a result expected from another person                                             | The dependency is not an action one can perform oneself                               |
| Someday/Maybe           | A possibility not currently committed                                                     | Different from an active obligation being avoided                                     |
| Tickler/future reminder | Information or an item to reconsider at a real future trigger                             | Not necessarily an action due on that date                                            |
| Reference               | Nonactionable information worth retaining                                                 | Different from an unresolved decision hidden in storage                               |

Independent next actions can coexist on a project; later dependent steps belong in support until actionable. A truly blocked project may have a tracked waiting dependency or future trigger rather than an invented executable next action. Track a follow-up action when it is appropriate.

## Principles and decision models

Externalize commitments into a trusted system, clarify outcomes and actions, keep reminders aligned with meaning, and review regularly. Trust comes from current, complete-enough reminders and dependable review, not from capturing every possible detail.

**Four criteria for choosing actions:** filter first by context (what is possible here), then available time, then available energy, then relative priority among feasible choices. Priority incorporates real commitments and perspective; universal high labels do not establish an execution order.

**Threefold nature of work:** doing predefined work, doing work as it shows up, and defining work. Clarifying a backlog is real work but should not consume all time intended for delivering commitments. An interruption can be chosen intentionally; its arrival alone does not make it highest priority.

**Horizons of Focus:** ground/actions, horizon 1/projects, horizon 2/Areas of focus and accountability, horizon 3/goals and objectives, horizon 4/vision, horizon 5/purpose and principles. Use higher horizons when priorities or capacity conflict. Indicative time spans for goals and vision are perspective aids, not required metadata or arbitrary deadlines.

**Natural Planning Model:** purpose and principles → outcome vision → brainstorming → organizing → identifying next actions. Use when a project needs thinking beyond its current next step. Planning depth should fit uncertainty and complexity; a simple project does not need elaborate supporting documents.

## Rules and conditions

- If no action is needed, choose reference, incubation, or a discard decision. Discarding is subject to the person's authority and retention needs.
- If action is needed, identify the successful outcome and next step. Track a project when more than one action is required.
- During clarification, a suitable action taking roughly two minutes can be done immediately when authorized and appropriate. This is an efficiency heuristic; adjust to the context rather than impose a stopwatch test.
- Delegate only when appropriate and authorized; retain a Waiting For reminder. Otherwise defer one's own action to a next-action list or a genuine date-specific calendar reminder.
- Every active project needs a current route forward: a real next action, explicit waiting dependency, or future trigger. Review blocked items rather than disguising them with fake actions.

## Lifecycle and theoretical status

A commitment can be captured, clarified, accepted, organized, acted on, delegated/blocked, reviewed, completed, renegotiated, canceled, or explicitly incubated. State changes follow real agreements and outcomes, not merely moving reminders. Completion and cancellation are different; a paused commitment needs an explicit review/reactivation decision.

GTD is a practitioner methodology, not a collection of mathematical theorems. Cognitive offloading and clear plans are plausible mechanisms supported by some research, but this skill does not claim guaranteed stress reduction, clinical effects, or a universal working-memory limit. The two-minute heuristic and typical project horizon are practical guidance. Local audit levels and recovery timeboxes are implementation policies, not additional GTD laws.
