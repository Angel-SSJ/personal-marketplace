# GTD audits and preventive checks

## Scope and evidence

Audit commitment flow using a bounded set of real open loops and reminders. State which inboxes, projects, waiting items, calendar period, or review episode was inspected. Unseen work is unknown. Do not claim a complete trusted system from one cleaned list.

Evaluate reminder meaning, decisions, and workflow, not filename conventions or mandatory content inside supporting documents. An action's clarity can be assessed from the person's explanation; no prescribed task-file schema is required.

## Local audit levels

- **In-use check:** When completing or reviewing an action, check outcome status and the real next action/dependency.
- **Focused audit:** Inspect a stalled project, repeated missed follow-up, unreliable calendar reminder, or recurring inbox bottleneck.
- **System audit:** Broaden scope when focused repairs fail, missed obligations span several responsibilities, or a major transition changes commitments.

These audit levels are local preventive policy. The full Weekly Review remains a core GTD practice. Do not replace it with recurring micro-audits or schedule a second heavy review of the same material without cause.

## Audit checks

| Step or handoff | Evidence question                                               | Defect if confirmed                                                                          | Minimum repair                                        |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Capture         | Are important open loops reliably collected and revisited?      | Real commitments are repeatedly lost before clarification                                    | Restore a reliable capture/revisit path               |
| Clarify         | Is the outcome distinguished from the next action?              | A vague project is presented as executable work                                              | Clarify that item's finish and next step              |
| Organize        | Does each reminder reflect its real meaning?                    | A wish becomes a deadline, reference hides an obligation, or a waiting item looks executable | Route by actual state                                 |
| Projects        | Does active work have a current route forward?                  | No action, dependency, or future trigger exists                                              | Identify the missing decision or blocker              |
| Waiting For     | Are delegated results and follow-up visible?                    | An expected result disappears after delegation                                               | Restore the waiting reminder and review cue           |
| Calendar        | Are date/time claims real and reliable?                         | Arbitrary dates obscure actual commitments                                                   | Separate real constraints from optional targets       |
| Reflect         | Was the full Weekly Review actually performed?                  | Only a quick sweep was called a complete review                                              | Complete or explicitly schedule the missing parts     |
| Engage          | Do choices respect context, time, energy, and priority?         | Impossible or low-value choices repeatedly displace real obligations                         | Filter feasible actions and renegotiate capacity      |
| Closure         | Are completed, canceled, paused, and incubated states distinct? | Relabeling silently erases an accepted promise                                               | Confirm agreements and preserve remaining obligations |

Priority labels, action counts, and age alone do not prove a defect. A blocked project can be legitimate if its dependency and review condition are visible. Avoid inventing a fake next action to make every project row appear compliant.

Distinguish GTD reminder validity from this user's active storage policy. A future-trigger-only project can be honestly tracked but does not qualify as an active 01-projects support container; that container needs Next Action or Waiting For. Surface the missing route or paused state without fabricating work or abandoning agreements. Check action/support links and archive transitions, not arbitrary file contents or project-name conventions.

## Findings and priorities

For each finding, give observed evidence, inspected scope, relevant GTD distinction, impact on actual work, smallest correction, verification step, and recurrence trigger. Mark inferred causes and missing facts. Prioritize imminent real obligations and lost agreements, then systemic ambiguity and stale reminders, then optional convenience improvements.

Recommend one manageable repair or a short ordered set. Do not manufacture a long remediation backlog for cosmetic issues. New improvement tasks are proposals until accepted; a methodology audit does not authorize changing the person's calendar or commitments.

## Verify and prevent recurrence

Replay the original failure: can the person see the expected result, find a feasible next action, or encounter the reminder at the right time? Check that no genuine obligation vanished during cleanup. Separate a proposed change from an executed and verified one.

Place a small cue at the point of failure: capture Waiting For at delegation, replenish next actions after progress, or check remaining obligations at project closure. Test the cue during normal use and the Weekly Review. Escalate only if it fails repeatedly, missed work remains consequential, or the system's scope changes.

If the evidence shows a usable personal adaptation, explain why no correction is needed. The goal is dependable choices and agreements, not uniform-looking lists.
