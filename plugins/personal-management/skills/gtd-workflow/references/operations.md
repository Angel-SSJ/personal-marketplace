# GTD operations, reviews, and recovery

## Capture and clarification pipeline

Input → what is it? → actionable? → desired outcome and next action if yes → organize reminders by meaning → review → choose and execute → update state.

Capture with minimal friction and reliable revisiting. Clarify one input at a time; do not leave the meaning undecided merely by moving it to a prettier collection.

| Condition                                                  | Decision and destination                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No action, no retention value                              | Propose discard; execute only within authority and retention constraints      |
| No action now, potential later commitment                  | Someday/Maybe, or a future reconsideration trigger                            |
| No action, useful information                              | Reference; organize support by current usefulness                             |
| Actionable and requires multiple actions                   | Track a finite outcome as a project and identify its current route forward    |
| Next action is suitable, authorized, and about two minutes | Do it during clarification if context permits; verify before marking complete |
| Another person owns the action/result                      | Delegate if authorized; track Waiting For and a review/follow-up trigger      |
| One's own action has genuine day/time specificity          | Calendar reminder; distinguish a real deadline from a preferred target        |
| One's own action has flexible timing                       | Next Actions, with context useful for selection                               |

Dates and owners must come from actual agreements or clearly labeled proposals. A real deadline can inform both a project reminder and an earlier next action; do not hide everything until the deadline arrives. Optional work reservations are planning aids, not new external deadlines. Do not infer which Friday or timezone a calendar write should use when that is unresolved.

## Project and delegation lifecycle

Apply the user's [storage architecture](storage-architecture.md) when support folders exist. An active container in 01-projects requires a real Next Action or Waiting For. GTD's general future-trigger route remains valid as reminder semantics, but future-trigger-only/paused support is not an active Projects container under this local profile. Clarify the commitment and nonactive support state rather than invent a task. Keep an undated accepted promise visible while clarifying its actual target; no arbitrary hard deadline is permitted.

Action reminders remain exclusively in the action system. Area standards and persistent records stay in 02-areas; finite multi-step outcomes/project drafts use 01-projects and its four-role layout. A single routine Area action does not require a fictional multi-step project. At closure preserve residual actions before moving the whole inactive support container to 04-archives/01-projects. Archive lookup is allowed, but editing requires reactivation; preserve frozen submissions while working in docs.

1. Confirm the desired outcome and whether it is currently committed. Split a continuing responsibility into finite projects only when there are actual outcomes to achieve.
2. Identify the next visible action or the real blocker. A thinking action can be concrete: inspect a process, draft options, or ask a specific question.
3. Keep subsequent dependent steps in support. Make independent feasible actions visible as needed.
4. For delegation, retain the expected result, responsible person, relevant agreement/date if known, and a review/follow-up trigger. These are reminder meanings, not required document sections. A proposed follow-up date is not a fabricated promise from the recipient.
5. After progress or a reply, update the next action, waiting state, and support. If the agreement changes, renegotiate explicitly.
6. At completion, check the actual outcome and residual obligations. Complete, transfer, or renegotiate those obligations visibly before clearing the active project. Archive inactive support independently.

## Engage with limited capacity

Check the calendar's real constraints, then filter actions by context, available time, and energy. Compare the feasible choices against priorities and higher horizons. If none is feasible, identify a small enabling action, define work briefly, renegotiate capacity, or rest where appropriate. Do not force execution to satisfy a productivity score.

Under overload, distinguish committed work from possibilities, identify consequential constraints, and renegotiate scope or timing explicitly. Reducing a list by deleting genuine promises is not clarification. Twelve high labels do not establish which item should happen now.

## Full Weekly Review

Restore a trusted system through **Get Clear, Get Current, Get Creative**. Use a regular weekly appointment suited to the person; this is a review practice, not a guarantee of a particular duration.

**Get Clear:** collect loose inputs and relevant materials; process inboxes by deciding what they mean; empty the head through a mind sweep. Empty means clarified and routed, not all resulting work completed.

**Get Current:** review next actions; review past calendar entries for follow-ups; review upcoming calendar commitments for preparation; review Waiting For; review each active project for status and a real next action/dependency/trigger; review relevant checklists. Verify that changed agreements and support references remain coherent.

**Get Creative:** review Someday/Maybe for promotion, retention, or removal decisions; consider new possibilities and useful ideas. Accept new commitments deliberately and in light of capacity.

Do quick clarification-sized actions when appropriate, but avoid spending the review executing whole projects. Capture newly discovered work and return to the review. A recurring missed review is a workflow defect to investigate, not a reason for guilt or an automatic mandate for more audits.

## Recovery when the system has fallen behind

Use a bounded recovery check: protect imminent real commitments, capture the most distracting open loops, clarify one consequential project, surface its next action or waiting trigger, and schedule/retain the full Weekly Review. Name the remaining unreviewed scope. This restores a foothold; it is not evidence that the full review was completed.

At a major change in role or capacity, revisit Areas and higher horizons as well as actions. A broader strategic review can be useful when daily choices cannot resolve conflicting goals. Its cadence is a personal operating policy rather than a substitute for weekly maintenance.

Use the storage profile's monthly Area review to identify genuinely finite outcomes; assess Resources on reuse and archive retention annually without automatic deletion. Fold project-to-action linkage checks into the Weekly Review rather than adding duplicate heavy audits.

## Worked example

“Prepare conference” suggests a multi-action project; its outcome and next physical action need clarification. A delegated budget estimate belongs on Waiting For, with a follow-up proposed if none was agreed. “Learn painting” stays Someday/Maybe unless the person commits. A dentist appointment Friday at 10 is calendar-specific; confirm the exact date/time details before a write. The twelve priority items have no stated deadlines, so do not invent any. With twenty minutes and low energy, choose a feasible low-effort action after checking context and actual consequences; a short follow-up may fit, but is conditional on the person's situation.

## Common corrections

| Problem                                                  | Repair                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vague noun/intention on an action list                   | Clarify outcome and next visible step                            |
| Project without a route forward                          | Find real next action, waiting dependency, or future trigger     |
| Calendar flooded with wishes                             | Separate genuine date-specific commitments from optional targets |
| Delegated work disappears                                | Restore Waiting For tracking and follow-up                       |
| Incubation hides an accepted promise                     | Renegotiate rather than silently relabel                         |
| Inbox repeatedly becomes an execution queue              | Separate clarification from extended doing                       |
| Completing an action is mistaken for finishing a project | Recheck outcome and replenish the route forward                  |
